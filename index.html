How to photograph your sample

  1. Even, bright lighting, no shadows or glare. Natural daylight near a window works best -- avoid a single overhead lamp or flash, which throws a shadow across the page.
  2. In focus and sharp. A blurry photo is the single most common reason a sample fails to process -- tap to focus on the page before shooting, and hold steady.
  3. Camera flat and parallel to the page, not at an angle -- keeps every letter true to its real shape instead of stretched by perspective.
  4. Space your letters out and don't let strokes touch. Our software finds each letter by looking for a clear gap around it -- crowded or joined-up (cursive-style) writing can get merged into one shape or missed.
  5. Write at a normal size on a flat, unfolded page, with solid, consistent pen pressure -- very faint or scratchy strokes can be lost against the paper.
  6. A fine, consistent-width pen (a fineliner or a fine ballpoint, not a thick marker) traces most cleanly -- a thick or bleeding line blurs the exact edge of each letter.

See it for yourself

Your custom font is a careful re-creation of your handwriting, not a perfect trace of it -- and a small amount of natural variation is added on purpose so plotted pages don't look robotically repeated. Between that, your photo's lighting/angle/focus, and the physical pen and paper used on the day, your plotted result will be close to your handwriting but should not be expected to match your original sample 100%.
